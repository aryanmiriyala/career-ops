# LinkedIn And Referral Outreach Template

Use this when drafting LinkedIn DMs, email introductions, referral asks, recruiter
replies, hiring-manager notes, and coffee-chat follow-ups for a specific role.

This is the standalone outreach guide. It is deliberately separate from the `start-app`
skill, which covers the resume and cover-letter pipeline only.

## Core Principle: Proportional To The Channel

The most common mistake is sending a cover letter as a DM. Match the effort and length
to how the recipient will actually read the message.

| Stage | Target length | What it carries |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn connection request | 2-3 sentences, no link | Who you are, why them specifically |
| First DM after connecting | 3-5 short sentences | One proof point, one clear ask |
| Referral ask | 4-6 short sentences | One proof point, role link, direct ask |
| Follow-up | 1-2 sentences | A nudge and an easy out |
| Recruiter reply | 3-5 sentences | Direct answers, availability, next step |

An initial message may carry **one** compact proof point plus Aryan's site or resume
link. Not three. If it needs a second paragraph to explain fit, it is too long for the
channel and belongs in the application itself.

## Rules

- Match the channel using the table above.
- Lead with the recipient, not with Aryan. Reference their work, team, post, or the
  specific role before asking for anything.
- Include one concrete proof point that a human can picture, not a stack list. "Published
  an ICSME LLM-evaluation pipeline across 1,450 PRs" lands. "Skilled in Python, AWS,
  and React" does not.
- Keep the ask small and easy to say yes to: a referral, a brief chat, or whether they
  are open to connecting. Do not ask for a job, an interview, or a favor stack.
- Follow-ups are shorter than the first message and lower pressure. Give the recipient
  an easy out.
- Do not repeat Aryan's work history, tech stack, publications, or project list in a
  follow-up unless the recipient asked for more detail or a new detail materially
  changes the conversation.
- Give a timeline only when it is real. Do not invent urgency.
- Avoid desperation. No `I'd really appreciate the chance`, `please consider me`, or
  `if you think my background is good enough`.
- No semicolons and no em dashes. Keep it sayable out loud in one breath.

## Human Voice Gate

Drafted messages are written content that goes out under Aryan's name, so they follow the
same human-voice standard as application answers. Before returning or saving a message,
check it against every item below.

- Reads like something Aryan would say to a person, not like a generated cover-letter
  excerpt.
- Grounded in verified experience, real company research, or a real shared connection.
  Nothing invented.
- No generic praise (`great company`, `amazing work`), no unsupported personal feelings
  (`I have always been passionate about...`), and no flattery that could apply to any
  employer.
- No AI-sounding filler: `at the intersection of`, `deeply passionate`, `ever-evolving
  landscape`, `seamlessly`, `leverage`, `robust`, `transformative`, `perfect fit`,
  `cutting-edge`, `innovative solutions`.
- No colons in message prose unless a form field requires them.
- No semicolons and no em dashes.
- No phrase that would embarrass Aryan if the recipient screenshotted it.

## Message Shapes

Replace every placeholder with something specific. If a placeholder cannot be filled
truthfully, drop that sentence rather than leaving it generic.

### Connection Request

```text
Hi <Name>, I'm applying for <role> at <company>. Your work on <specific team, product,
or post> caught my attention. My background includes <one proof point>. I'd value
connecting.
```

### First DM After Connecting

```text
Hi <Name>, thanks for connecting. I applied for the <role> role on <team>. <One
sentence of specific, researched context about the team or the recipient's work.> My
closest experience is <one proof point with a number or concrete outcome>. If you have
15 minutes, I'd appreciate hearing what matters most for someone joining. My work is at
<site or resume link>.
```

### Referral Ask

```text
Hi <Name>, I applied for the <role> role at <company>. <One line connecting Aryan to
the work or product, specific enough that it could not be copy-pasted to another
company.> Would you be open to a referral? Role link: <link>. My portfolio is
<site or resume link if useful>. Happy to hop on a quick virtual coffee if that works.
```

### Follow-Up

```text
Hi <Name>, just following up on my note about the <role> opening. I'd still value a
brief conversation if your schedule allows. Either way, I appreciate <specific thing
they share or built>.
```

### Slightly Warmer Coffee-Chat Follow-Up

```text
Hi <Name>, just following up here. Would be glad to hear more about <company/team/role>
if you're open to a quick coffee chat.
```

### Recruiter Reply

Answer what was asked, confirm availability plainly, and propose the next step. Do not
re-pitch the resume unless asked.

## Optional Context To Collect Before Drafting

Ask only when the message would be materially weaker or risky without the answer:

- Who is the recipient, and what is the exact connection path (alumni, shared employer,
  same team, prior interaction)?
- What specific thing about their work, team, or the product should the message
  reference?
- Is this a cold outreach, a warm intro, a follow-up, or a reply?
- What is the one proof point that best matches this role and recipient?

## Verification Before Sending

- Length matches the channel table.
- Exactly one proof point in an initial message.
- The ask is small and specific.
- At least one detail that could not be sent to a different company.
- No banned filler, no semicolons, no em dashes.
- Recipient name, company, role, and links are all correct.
