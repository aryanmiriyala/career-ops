# LinkedIn And Referral Outreach Template

Use this when drafting LinkedIn DMs, email introductions, referral asks, recruiter
replies, hiring-manager notes, and coffee-chat follow-ups for a specific role.

This is the standalone outreach guide. It is deliberately separate from the `start-app`
skill, which covers the resume and cover-letter pipeline only.

## Start From Aryan's Own Voice

Aryan's best messages are the ones he wrote himself. The reference standard is his own
message to a contact at West Monroe, which the approved Quo message was modeled on:

> Hey <Name>! I hope you are well. I am a recent masters grad in computer science and I
> am currently working as a software engineer at an AI startup. I saw a job posting for
> a software engineering role at <company> that I am closely aligned with and very
> interested in. I was hoping to see if you would be able to provide me with a referral.
> The link to the job is below. Here's the link to my website if you'd like to get more
> insight about my experience. Also, happy to hop on a quick coffee chat if that would
> help as well.

Read that before drafting. Match its plainness, its shortness, and its low pressure. Most
drafts fail because they drift away from this voice, not because they are too brief.

## Core Principle: Talk About What You Are Building, Not Your Stack

The single biggest failure mode is a message that reads like a resume summary. Aryan
rejected repeated drafts for exactly this. The rule:

- Describe **what you are trying to build** and the **general nature of the work**, in
  plain words. "I build developer tools using AI workflows" is right.
- Do **not** name frameworks, languages, databases, internal tools, or product names
  unless Aryan asks for them.
- Do **not** spell out the company's full name or bolt "AI-powered" onto everything. It
  reads robotic.
- Do **not** list individual projects or the details of past work. One plain sentence
  about the kind of work is enough.
- Do **not** invent a mission connection. "Their mission around small businesses really
  resonates with me" was flagged as fake. Only say something about the company if Aryan
  actually has that connection. Otherwise, say you have been following them and leave it
  there.

## Core Principle: Proportional To The Channel

The second most common mistake is sending a cover letter as a DM. Match the effort and
length to how the recipient will actually read the message.

| Stage | Target length | What it carries |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn connection request | 2-3 sentences, no link | Who you are, why them specifically |
| First DM after connecting | 3-5 short sentences | One plain sentence on your work, one clear ask |
| Referral ask | 4-6 short sentences | Who you are now, the role, the job link, direct ask |
| Follow-up | 1-2 sentences | A nudge and an easy out |
| Recruiter reply | 3-5 sentences | Direct answers, availability, next step |

If a message needs a second paragraph to explain fit, it is too long for the channel and
belongs in the application itself.

## Rules

- Start from the reference message above. Keep the greeting, the plainness, and the
  closing.
- Match the channel length using the table above.
- Lead with the recipient, then who you are, then the role, then the ask. This is the
  order Aryan uses.
- Keep the ask small and direct. "Would you be open to a referral?" and "Would you be
  open to a quick chat?" are both fine. Do not ask for a job, an interview, or a stack of
  favors.
- Give them an easy out. The research is clear that a referral puts the referrer's
  reputation on the line, so make it easy to decline without awkwardness.
- Include the job link. Have the exact title and link ready so they never have to go
  looking. This is the one piece of homework that always belongs in the message.
- Offer the portfolio only when it adds value. Pause and ask whether it is even needed.
  Aryan questioned its inclusion in a draft where it did not help.
- Offer a **virtual** coffee chat only. Not in person.
- Follow-ups are shorter than the first message and lower pressure.
- Do not repeat Aryan's work history, stack, projects, or publications in a follow-up.
- Give a timeline only when it is real. Do not invent urgency.
- Avoid desperation. No `I'd really appreciate the chance`, `please consider me`, or
  `if you think my background is good enough`.
- Get the facts right. Confirm the current title and employer before sending. Aryan was
  still being described as an intern after he had started as a software engineer.

## Human Voice Gate

Drafted messages go out under Aryan's name, so they follow the same human-voice standard
as application answers. Read every draft against this list before returning or saving it.

- Reads like something Aryan would say out loud to a person, not like a generated
  cover-letter excerpt.
- Grounded in verified experience and real company research. Nothing invented.
- No technical depth. No stack names, no tool names, no project detail.
- No invented mission resonance or company flattery.
- No generic praise (`great company`, `amazing work`) and no unsupported personal
  feelings (`I have always been passionate about...`).
- No AI-sounding filler: `at the intersection of`, `deeply passionate`, `ever-evolving
  landscape`, `seamlessly`, `leverage`, `robust`, `transformative`, `perfect fit`,
  `cutting-edge`, `innovative solutions`.
- No colons in message prose unless a form field requires them.
- No semicolons and no em dashes.
- No phrase that would embarrass Aryan if the recipient screenshotted it.

If a draft is longer than six short sentences and is not a recruiter reply, cut it.

## Message Shapes

Replace every placeholder with something specific. If a placeholder cannot be filled
truthfully, drop that sentence rather than leaving it generic. The shapes below mirror the
approved Quo message.

### Connection Request

```text
Hi <Name>, I'm applying for <role> at <company>. Your work on <specific team, product,
or post> caught my attention. My background includes <one plain sentence on the work you
do>. I'd value connecting.
```

### First DM After Connecting

```text
Hi <Name>, thanks for connecting. I <applied for / am interested in> the <role> role on
<team>. My closest work is <one plain sentence on what you build, no stack>. Would you be
open to a quick chat? Happy to share more if it's useful.
```

### Referral Ask

```text
Hey <Name>! Hope you're doing well. I'm a recent MS grad in computer science and currently
working as a software engineer at an AI startup called <company>. I spend my days using AI
coding tools to build <plain description of the tools you build>.

I saw the <role> role at <company> and it's closely aligned with what I've been doing.
Been following <company> since <real reason> and would love to be considered. Would you be
open to a referral? Job link: <link>

My portfolio if helpful: <link>

Also happy to hop on a quick virtual coffee if that works.

Thanks!
```

### Follow-Up

```text
Hi <Name>, just following up on my note about the <role> opening. I'd still value a brief
conversation if your schedule allows. Either way, I appreciate <specific thing they share
or built>.
```

### Slightly Warmer Coffee-Chat Follow-Up

```text
Hi <Name>, just following up here. Would be glad to hear more about <company/team/role>
if you're open to a quick virtual coffee chat.
```

### Recruiter Reply

Answer what was asked, confirm availability plainly, and propose the next step. Do not
re-pitch the resume unless asked.

## Optional Context To Collect Before Drafting

Ask only when the message would be materially weaker or risky without the answer:

- Who is the recipient, and what is the exact connection path (alumni, shared employer,
  same team, prior interaction)?
- Is there a real reason Aryan has been following the company? If not, do not invent one.
- Is this a cold outreach, a warm intro, a follow-up, or a reply?
- Is the current title and employer correct, and is the job link exact?

## Verification Before Sending

- Reads like Aryan's reference message, not a resume summary.
- No stack names, tool names, project names, or "AI-powered" explanations.
- No invented mission resonance or company flattery.
- Length matches the channel table.
- The ask is small, direct, and easy to decline.
- The job title and link are exact and current.
- Coffee chat is virtual only, and the portfolio is only included if it earns its place.
- No banned filler, no colons, no semicolons, no em dashes.
- Recipient name, company, role, and links are all correct.
