## Relevant Findings

- Smartsheet describes itself as a work-management platform used by a large enterprise customer base, including 85% of the Fortune 500.
- The careers page emphasizes engineering and developer roles, product management partnership, global offices, and flexible work options depending on role and team requirements.
- Smartsheet's interview page says recruiter screens cover work authorization, compensation, and availability, so work-authorization questions should be prepared but kept out of submitted artifacts.
- The pasted job description aligns with Smartsheet's public AI/work-management direction: full-stack services, distributed systems, AWS, React/TypeScript, AI-assisted development, architectural discussions, and product collaboration.
- Third-party sponsor-history data from Ellis reports recent Smartsheet H-1B filings, including Software Engineer I records in 2025 and 2026. This is useful risk context, not a guarantee that this specific role supports future sponsorship.
- E-Verify/USCIS guidance confirms STEM OPT extension employment requires employer E-Verify participation and Form I-983 training-plan support. No Smartsheet E-Verify confirmation was found in the pasted posting, so this remains a recruiter follow-up.

## Package Implications

- Use Smartsheet's exact role and engineering language in truthful context: Software Engineer I (Remote Eligible), full-stack services, distributed systems, cloud infrastructure, AWS, React, TypeScript, REST APIs, AI-assisted development, code review, debugging, and architectural discussion.
- Do not claim Kotlin, Java, Kubernetes, Azure, or direct Smartsheet-scale high-availability SaaS ownership without verified source material.
- Cover letter should connect SmartSolve's internal workflow tooling and AI-assisted development to Smartsheet's human-team-plus-AI-agent direction, without overstating direct product familiarity.
- Submitted artifacts should not mention F-1, OPT, STEM OPT, sponsorship, E-Verify, or Form I-983.
