### 1. What do you hope to get out of HackUTD?
I spent most of my first hackathon in 2023 trying to figure out how to make a Flutter map actually render a campus route. It was messy, but it changed how I saw software -- moving from just passing assignments to designing systems that people actually use. I spend my day-to-day work now building backend data pipelines and AI gateways, which can feel detached from the end user. HackUTD is massive, and I want to use the pressure of a 24-hour deadline to build a prototype for a real, confusing problem alongside other engineers who care about making complex systems usable.

### 2. Tell us about the coolest thing you've built, or something you want to build.
I recently built an answer engine for my university that turns scattered campus PDFs and webpages into a searchable graph. The hardest part wasn't the AI -- it was writing a C++ crawler that didn't constantly crash when it hit weirdly formatted DOCX files from 2015. I ended up building thread-safe queues to collect the resources before passing them to Python for cleaning and FAISS for ranking. I loved this project because it wasn't just wrapping an API call. It forced me to handle low-level data ingestion and give it enough structure so that a student could actually trust the Next.js interface.

### 3. Describe a situation where you had to learn a new tool or technology on a very tight deadline.
During RocketHacks, my team decided to build a grading platform that parsed student submissions and generated feedback using LangChain and Mistral AI. I had about 24 hours to figure out how to make the pipeline consistently return structured rubric breakdowns instead of hallucinated text. I ended up ignoring the quick-start tutorials and just read the source code to understand how the models were chunking the PDF inputs. It was stressful when the parser kept failing on weird HTML tables, but getting that working model integrated into our Express backend taught me how to break down an unfamiliar technology fast. We ended up winning two MLH tracks.
